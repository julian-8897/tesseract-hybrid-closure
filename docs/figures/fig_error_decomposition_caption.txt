Mode-wise decomposition of the endpoint-field squared vorticity error, averaged over all 32 validation seeds, at rollout steps 30, 120 and 500. For one Fourier mode with model coefficient a and filtered-DNS coefficient b, the identity |a-b|^2 = (|a|-|b|)^2 + 2|a||b|(1-cos(arg a - arg b)) splits the error exactly into an amplitude term, from getting the mode's magnitude wrong, and a phase term, from getting its position wrong. Both terms are non-negative and, summed over every shell, they return the mean squared vorticity difference itself, so this decomposes the endpoint field MSE at each requested step rather than introducing a new metric. Top row: total squared error per shell, sharing one vertical scale across steps. Bottom row: the phase share of that error, where 1.0 means the mode's magnitude is right and only its phase is wrong. Shells are shown for 1 <= k <= 32; the sums in the report keep every shell so the identity holds exactly. This mode-wise reading of errors through their magnitude and phase parts parallels the closed-form Fourier-multiplier analysis in Köhler's thesis ("From Numerical Simulators of PDEs to Neural Emulators and Back", 2026, arXiv:2608.24547), which applies to linear schemes; this system is nonlinear in both the advection and the closure, so the decomposition here is its empirical counterpart. Diagnostic only: validation split, and no reported metric, model selection, or test-split result depends on it.
